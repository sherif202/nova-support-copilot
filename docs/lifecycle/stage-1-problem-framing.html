Problem Framing

Is AI the right fit for Nova's contact center in Egypt? Answered with the Triple Fit canvas from your strategy exercises, grounded in public evidence from Egypt and Nova's own brief, then turned into an AI use case and user stories.

Phase 1 of 4 · Frame & Prepare Do: Is AI even the right fit? Weigh the cost of being wrong. Catch: Most ideas don't need AI. Be honest.
Verdict · go, narrowly, with conditions

AI fits one job: reading a free-text question in Egyptian Arabic, English or Franco-Arabic and matching it to this customer's own transaction and the right policy rule.

Everything around that job is code, buttons or a person. The weakest fit is incentive: agents, operations and compliance each have reasons to resist, so the launch plan has to answer "what's in it for them" before it answers "how good is the model".

The opportunity space is defined by a gap between explanation and access. Egyptians increasingly bank digitally, but when money moves unexpectedly they can't get an explanation without waiting for a person, and the bots that exist don't visibly ground their answers in the customer's own records. Part B · Triple Fit AI Strategy Canvas

Market fit, solution fit, incentive fit

From Exercise 2 of your document. Each column answers its key questions, names its red flags and gives a score from 1 to 5.

1 · Market fit4/5
Problem significance
27,000 repeat contacts a month, +15% a year. Reaching a person is the top public complaint.
Market readiness
77.6% inclusion and 16M+ InstaPay users show digital habits. App trust is low (CIB app 2.8★), and Nova's app is used only for balances.
Competitive landscape
CIB, NBE and Banque Misr already run assistants. Room remains for grounded, cited, Franco-aware answers.
Regulatory environment
Banking Law 194/2020 makes secrecy breaches a crime (Art. 231: at least 1 year and EGP 200,000–500,000). The data protection law 151/2020 leaves banks to CBE rules. CBE outsourcing rules apply to each AI provider, and Stage 3 plans two (a primary and a backup vendor). No AI-specific CBE rule found (not proof there is none).
Red flags
  • Sending customer data to a provider outside Egypt needs CBE sign-off.
  • "Another chatbot" is not a differentiator on its own.
  • Callers may not be app users.
2 · Solution fit4/5
Technical feasibility
Classifying and explaining 3 intents from fixed rules is a strength of current models. Egyptian dialect in Latin script is the known weak spot: researchers built Nile-Chat for exactly that.
Data availability
The brief provides about 20 policy documents, transactions per customer and 200 tickets to label, all synthetic. A labelled test set still has to be built in stage 2. Real records need a read-only core-banking link.
Alternative (non-AI) approaches
A status screen, a card flow and expiry alerts solve part of it. Fees in free text need AI (see Do 1 below).
Augmentation vs automation
Automate information answers for 3 intents. Augment agents on everything else with a handoff summary. Never automate money, waivers or dispute decisions.
Red flags
  • Fraud recall is untested, and one miss costs a customer money.
  • Franco accuracy is untested on real phrasing.
3 · Incentive fit3/5
User incentives
Customers: answers at 2 a.m. in their own words, and a person one tap away. Threat: distrust of bots after bad app experiences. Agents: fewer repetitive calls. Threat: fear for their jobs.
Organizational incentives
Absorbs the 15% growth without hiring ≈18 agents a year, and gives the app a reason to exist beyond balances.
Implementation incentives
Contact-center ops adopt it, but handle time rises when easy calls leave. Compliance must sign off and is rewarded for saying no. IT must open a read-only data link.
Red flags
  • Agent KPIs punish the change unless they're reset.
  • Compliance can veto late; bring them in at stage 1.
  • Pitch it as "no new hires for growth", not "fewer agents".

The costs are lopsided. A false alarm costs one agent contact, which happens today anyway. A missed fraud case costs a customer's money, and a data leak is a crime under the Banking Law. So when unsure, the copilot hands off, and fraud recall, not overall accuracy, is the number that matters.

Catch

Most ideas don't need AI. Be honest.

Doesn't need AI: build it without

  • Expiring cards. Already automatic (CRD-2). A push alert 30 days before expiry stops the question.
  • "Where is my dispute?" A status screen plus the SMS that DSP-7 already sends.
  • Damaged card. A two-tap flow: request, pick delivery, see the EGP 100 fee.
  • Spotting fraud. A word list in 3 languages, in code. Faster, cheaper, testable to 100%.
  • Anything that moves money or decides. A person, always.

Needs AI: the narrow part we build

  • Understanding the question as typed. "masareef", "et5asam", "el visa" and "14/9" mapped to the right row and rule.
  • Explaining this charge. "Your EGP 75 is the minimum for a withdrawal abroad, because 3% of 2,000 is 60" (FEE-2.4).
  • Telling look-alikes apart. "badal fa2ed" (lost) from "et2sar" (damaged); a dispute from fraud.
  • Answering in the customer's language, with the source shown.

Honest conclusion: part of the 27,000 can be removed with no AI, through the status screen, the card flow and expiry alerts. Build those alongside the copilot and credit them separately, so the copilot's number isn't inflated. Reframe trigger: if the interview shows most of these contacts need an action rather than an answer, reframe as an agent assistant before building.

Exercise 4

AI user stories

Explain my charge

As a Nova customer, I want to ask about a charge in my own words, so that I understand it without calling.

  • Finds the matching transaction and cites the fee rule
  • Every amount in the answer appears in a source
  • Intent accuracy ≥ 90% on the eval set
  • Reply in ≤ 4 s (p95)
  • Handles Franco, Arabic and English; replies to Franco in Arabic script

Data: structured (transactions) + text (policies). Technique: LLM classification and grounded generation. Ethics: no advice, no selling; explain, don't persuade.

Fraud goes to a person, first

As a customer who sees a payment I didn't make, I want to reach the Fraud team at once, so that no more money leaves my account.

  • Fraud recall 100% on all fraud cases, including "badal fa2ed"
  • Block-card button and hotline in the first reply, under 1 s
  • Never asks for or repeats a card number, PIN or OTP
  • Mixed messages (fee + fraud) go to fraud

Data: word list in 3 languages, in code. Technique: rules before the model. Ethics: bias toward false alarms over misses.

Hand over with context

As a support agent, I want to receive handed-off chats with a summary and the intent, so that I don't make the customer repeat themselves.

  • Every handoff has intent, priority, masked chat and a reference number
  • Correct queue: Fraud, Complaints or Agent
  • Agents rate the summary useful in > 80% of cases
  • No AI-written text reaches the customer from the inbox without the agent's approval

Data: chat log, ticket. Technique: LLM summary. Ethics: agents' KPIs are reset so harder remaining calls don't count against them.

Measure

How we'll know it's right

Decided now, before anything is built
Correctness and relevance
Retrieval quality, before generation
Did the lookup find the right transaction, dispute, rule or notice? Measured on the labelled set with no model call: Recall@3 ≥ 95% for records, citation recall and precision ≥ 90% for rules.
Groundedness
Every number and claim in the answer appears in what was retrieved.
Judge, calibrated
An LLM judge scores helpfulness against expert labels, checked against human scores first.
Safety
Hard gates
Fraud recall 100%. Harmless 1.00. All 12 red-team attacks blocked.
Rates to watch
Hallucination rate, policy-violation rate ("approved", "refunded"), PII leakage rate: all must be 0 on the eval set.
Caught twice
By input checks and output checks, so one miss isn't a leak.
Business outcome
North Star
Verified resolution rate. "Verified" guards against gaming: a chat only counts if the customer doesn't come back within 7 days.
Also
Handoff rate, kill-switch use, customer rating, cost per message and per conversation, retry rate, agent corrections, p95 reply time.
The loop
Every new failure in the pilot becomes a regression case in the eval set, and its fix goes into Nova's own files (a trap or an example), so it carries over to any model or vendor.

What we commit to, going into stage 2

UsersLogged-in Nova customers. Agents work the handoffs. Fraud team and Complaints unit get escalations.
Job to be done"When I have a question about my own fee, dispute or card, give me the right answer now, in my language. If something is wrong, get me a person."
In scopeDispute status and documents, fee explanation, card replacement (damaged, expiring).
Out of scopeMoving money, waivers, dispute decisions, credit and loans, advice, selling, anyone else's account.

Top 3 unproven assumptions and the fastest test: 1) Most contacts need information, not action: one contact-center interview, "of your last 20 calls, how many needed no action?" 2) Models handle real Franco phrasing: run the golden record's Franco cases in the stage 3 bake-off. 3) Agents and compliance will back it: a 30-minute session showing the cost matrix and the "no new hires" framing.
